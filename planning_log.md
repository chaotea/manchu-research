## Literature review — implications for the next experiment

- [Chung and Choi, ManchuAI-OCR](https://github.com/mic7ch1/ManchuAI-OCR) uses a 60,000-image subset of tyotakuki's synthetic dataset. Its published CRNN uses larger inputs and a deeper CNN/LSTM than ours, outputs Manchu script, and trains under a different schedule. Their successful synthetic recognition supports investigating our implementation and training setup before abandoning CRNNs. [Published paper](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/computational-humanities-research/article/finetuning-visionlanguage-models-as-ocr-systems-for-lowresource-languages-a-case-study-of-manchu/D67751520CFEC6E538F4FF5E1EAF5AA4).
- [SCC³](https://github.com/Hunt9160/SCC3) presents a specialized Manchu recognition architecture and MW14850. Its reported accuracy is not directly comparable without matching task, splits, and output units. [Paper](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.eswa.2025.129374).
- [Tyotakuki's ManchuOCR](https://github.com/tyotakuki/ManchuOCR) supplies the synthetic data and an attention-based sequence-recognition/page-preprocessing implementation. Preserve its authorship and attribution.

Our current training has not reproduced Chung and Choi's dataset or experiment: we used our smaller rendered dataset for training and tyotakuki only for external validation. Strong synthetic results in the literature also do not guarantee historical-document accuracy.
